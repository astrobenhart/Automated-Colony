# v0.8 Release Validation Report

Date: 2026-06-27
Branch: v0.8-development
Validation path: `SimulationRunner` -> `World.update()`

## Executive Summary

FAIL

The previous release validation results are superseded. They used direct `World.advance_day()` calls and did not exercise villager AI, movement, task execution, builder behaviour, residential construction, or household splitting.

The new headless validation runner executes every production simulation tick through `World.update()`. Under this valid path, the Residential Growth Model does activate:

- residential demand is generated
- house construction completes
- household splits occur
- succession continues
- families persist
- the Chronicle remains active
- no simulation stalls or resource deadlocks were observed

However, v0.8 is not yet release-ready. The 100-year runs show a consistent long-term population decline. One seed reached a population-collapse warning, and all three 100-year runs ended far below the starting population. This is now a real tick-faithful balance result, not a validation harness artifact.

Recommendation: Requires further development

## Population Trends

Observed pattern:

- Population grows or remains healthy through the first 15-30 years.
- Births slow substantially after early family expansion.
- Natural deaths accumulate after the founder generation ages.
- After year 50, populations trend steadily downward.

This suggests v0.8 now has a functioning generational loop, but the loop is not strong enough over a century. The issue appears to be balancing or lifecycle pressure rather than missing household formation.

## Economy

Food and water did not collapse.

- Final food remained positive in all six runs.
- Water storage grew very large in century-scale low-population runs.
- Housing capacity exceeded population in all final states.
- No homeless households were observed.

Economy conclusion: resource collapse is not causing the population decline. In late-century runs, resource abundance rises because the population has already fallen.

## Renderer

Validation ran headless, so renderer frame time was not sampled during the long suite.

The headless runner removes renderer, UI, frame limiting, and presentation delay only. It does not remove simulation work. Renderer validation should remain a separate visual/performance pass.

## Chronicle

The Chronicle remained active throughout long simulations.

- 50-year runs produced 659-706 entries.
- 100-year runs produced 1188-1239 entries.
- Entries included births, family events, succession, environmental history, and renewal milestones.

No Chronicle inactivity was detected. Chronicle volume is high but not deadlocked.

## Remaining Issues

Severity 1 - Release-blocking:

- Century-scale population renewal is not stable. Births do not replace natural deaths over 100 years under tick-faithful gameplay.

Severity 2 - Validation performance:

- Headless 50-year and 100-year runs miss the desired runtime targets on this machine. This does not invalidate gameplay, but it makes release validation slow.

Severity 3 - Balance observation:

- Water stockpiles can become extremely large after population decline. This appears to be a consequence of low demand rather than the cause of decline.

## Recommendation

Requires further development

Do not merge v0.8 into main yet.

Recommended next action:

Create a focused release-blocker issue for century-scale population renewal. Do not add new systems. Investigate why births slow after early household growth despite available housing, food, water, households, and families. Tune only after identifying whether the limiting factor is partnership availability, child spacing, dependent-child caps, adult age distribution, parent age limits, household rules, or birth scoring.
