# v0.8 Population Renewal Investigation

Date: 2026-06-28
Branch: v0.8-development
Investigation path: `SimulationRunner` -> `World.update()`

## Executive Summary

Century-scale population decline is caused primarily by a household-forming gap in the partnership pipeline.

The simulation forms partnerships, but later-life partnerships do not reliably become co-residential households. Births require partners to share a household. Household splitting also requires partnered adults to live in the same household. Once the founding generation ages out, the village increasingly contains partnered adults who are not living together, plus older partnerships no longer producing children. Birth attempts fall to zero even while food, water, housing capacity, families, succession, construction, and Chronicle systems continue working.

Primary cause:

- Partnerships are not consistently household-forming after both adults already belong to established households.

Secondary contributing causes:

- Active partner references appear permanent; death does not clear the surviving partner into the unpartnered pool.
- Parent age eligibility becomes restrictive as the paired adult population ages.
- Birth probability is low, but the evidence shows the larger issue is that eligible birth pairs disappear entirely by mid-century.

Non-causes:

- Food availability
- Water availability
- Housing capacity
- Residential construction
- Household splitting architecture
- Family persistence
- Chronicle activity

## Household Analysis

Households and residential systems are functioning:

- Household splits occurred: 8 total.
- Residential expansions occurred: 11 total.
- Housing capacity reached 90 while population declined to 10.
- Homeless households were not the observed blocker.
- Food and water remained available.

The issue is not that households cannot split or homes cannot be built. The issue is that later partnered adults often do not become a shared household, so they cannot enter either the birth pipeline or the split-household pipeline.

Relevant implementation evidence:

- `src.births.birth_eligible()` requires both parents to have the same `household_id`.
- `src.residential.household_split_candidates()` also requires partners to be occupants of the same household.
- `src.partnerships.prefer_shared_household()` moves partners together only when one partner has no household or one existing household has size <= 1.
- If both partners already live in established multi-person households, the partnership is recorded but no shared household is formed.

This is appropriate as a conservative first implementation, but it is too conservative for century-scale renewal.

## Family Analysis

Families persist and remain available for history:

- Families increased from the founding set to 12 in the 100-year probe.
- Chronicle entries continued through 1197 entries.
- Family generation depth reached the second generation but did not deepen further in the failing run.

Family identity is not disappearing. The lineage problem is upstream: too few later households produce children.

## Partnership Analysis

Partnerships exist, but the partnership pool stops renewing effectively.

Observed:

- Average partnerships remain non-zero through the whole century.
- Eligible new partnership candidates average zero after decade 11.
- Birth-eligible pair-days fall to zero by decade 41.
- Living-together failures rise from 3.61% in decade 1 to 100% from decade 61 onward.

Relevant implementation evidence:

- `is_unpartnered_adult()` requires `partner_id is None` and an empty `partner_ids` list.
- Natural death in `src.renewal.update_renewal()` marks the deceased villager dead but does not clear or reclassify the surviving partner's active partnership fields.
- There is no widow/widower or partnership-ended state.

This means death can remove a partner from the living population while leaving the survivor unavailable for new partnership formation. Even without implementing romance or breakups, death needs an explicit lifecycle transition for partnership references.

## Root Cause

Primary cause:

Partnerships do not reliably create or preserve co-residential family units after both adults already belong to established households.

Evidence:

- Birth eligibility requires shared household.
- Household split eligibility requires shared household.
- Partnership formation does not move partners together when both are already in multi-person households.
- Living-together failures climb to 78.24% in decade 51 and 100% from decade 61 onward.
- Birth attempts are zero from decade 41 onward despite abundant housing, food, and water.

Secondary contributing cause:

Partnership lifecycle has no death transition.

Evidence:

- Natural death does not clear surviving partner references.
- `is_unpartnered_adult()` excludes any adult with a `partner_id` or non-empty `partner_ids`.
- Eligible new partnership candidates average zero after decade 11.

Secondary contributing cause:

Parent age eligibility becomes restrictive while the founding generation dominates the active partnership pool.

Evidence:

- Parent-age blocked pair-days rise to 59.73% in decade 21 and 75.46% in decade 31.
- This is a consequence of poor partnership renewal and late co-residence, not necessarily an overly strict age rule by itself.

Non-causes:

- Housing capacity: 0% blocked in birth gates; final capacity 90 for population 10.
- Food: 0% blocked.
- Water: 0% blocked.
- Dependent-child cap: only 1.59%-6.02% blocked during early decades.
- Child spacing: meaningful early, but not responsible for zero attempts after decade 41.
- Residential construction: homes and splits occurred before the decline.
- Chronicle: remained active.

## Recommended Changes

Highest confidence:

1. Make partnerships household-forming when they are intended to support renewal.

   When two adult partners are both in established households, the simulation needs a household path. Options include:

   - create a new household request for the pair
   - allow one partner to join the other's household when capacity allows
   - create residential demand for a partnered pair that cannot cohabit

   This should remain a household simulation rule, not romance drama, marriage, or legal family structure.

2. Add a partnership lifecycle transition on death.

   When a partner dies, the survivor should no longer be blocked forever by an active `partner_id`. The system can preserve memory/history while clearing active partnership eligibility. This is not a breakup mechanic; it is lifecycle cleanup.

Medium confidence:

3. Review partnership candidate availability after family/direct-relative filtering.

   Once widowed survivors and co-residence are fixed, confirm whether direct-family exclusion is too restrictive in small late-century populations.

Lower confidence until primary fixes are validated:

4. Review birth probability and child spacing.

   The early decades produce births, but the probability is low. Do not tune it first. Re-run the investigation after fixing co-residence and partner lifecycle cleanup; only then decide whether probability or spacing needs adjustment.
